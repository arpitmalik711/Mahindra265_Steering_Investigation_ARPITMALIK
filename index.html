01 — Project Overview

Why Steering Geometry Matters

Steering geometry defines how a vehicle's front wheels turn, how they are oriented relative to the road, and how effortlessly the driver can control direction. The kingpin inclination (KPI), camber, scrub radius, and the differential angle relationship between the two front wheels all interact to determine steering feel, tyre wear, and stability.

Agricultural tractors operate at low speeds in high-slip soil, where geometry deviations affect tyre scrub, driver fatigue, and headland efficiency differently from highway vehicles. This study fully characterises the Mahindra 265's steering geometry through field measurement, photogrammetry, and CAD reconstruction.

Methodology: field survey, plumb-line photogrammetry, and smartphone compass sweep — a low-cost but rigorous approach to full steering geometry characterisation applicable to any production vehicle.

06 — Theoretical Steering Model

Ideal Wheel Angle Relationship

The ideal inner wheel angle δi as a function of outer wheel angle δo, wheelbase L, and track t — derived purely from the geometry of concentric circular arcs:

cot(δi) = cot(δo) − t / L
WHERE: L = 1905 mm  |  t = 1270 mm

The cotangent relationship is non-linear — the ideal curve progressively diverges from parallel steering as lock increases.

13 — Measurement Uncertainty

Sources of Error and Limitations

📐
Smartphone Sensor Accuracy

Consumer MEMS accelerometers: ±0.3–0.5° resolution. Averaging reduces random error but leaves an irreducible ±0.5° uncertainty band on all reported angles.

📷
Photogrammetry Assumptions

CAD reconstruction assumes perpendicular camera alignment and zero lens distortion. Perspective error contributes ~±0.5° to KPI measurement.

🔄
Tyre Deformation Effects

Sidewall deformation under load alters apparent wheel plane orientation. High-aspect-ratio agricultural tyres can contribute up to ±1° apparent camber variation.

16 — Engineering Conclusion

Summary of Findings

Steering geometry of the Mahindra 265 (2012) was fully characterised through dimensional survey, SolidWorks photogrammetry reconstruction, and smartphone compass field measurements across the complete lock range.

The inner-to-outer wheel angle relationship (L=1905 mm, t=1270 mm) is divergent — near-ideal at low lock, with the outer wheel angle increasingly overshooting the geometric requirement as lock increases, reaching a ~16° excess at full lock, consistent with the steering arm convergence point lying forward of the rear axle.

KPI 7.75°, camber +3.0°, estimated scrub radius ~32 mm. The full geometry package is well-suited to low-speed, high-slip agricultural field operation and is consistent with the beam-axle packaging constraints of production tractor design.

This study is to be continued — next up: graph comparison and Ackermann percentage of the steering system.
